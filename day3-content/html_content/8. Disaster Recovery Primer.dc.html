High availability, backup and disaster recovery: staying up, and getting back

Three different tools for three different failures. High availability rides through a zone failure. Backup undoes bad data. Disaster recovery moves the platform to another region. None of them replaces the others.

LEARNING OBJECTIVE Describe high availability, backup and disaster recovery; compare managed and self-built DR; define RTO, RPO and replication approaches; and outline DR runbooks and deploying a secondary region as code.
01

Why: failures come in different sizes

Every data platform will eventually lose a machine, a zone, a table or a whole region. The question for each workload is how long the business can wait, and how much recent data it can afford to lose. The answer decides which protection you need and how much you spend on it.

03

RTO, RPO and replication approaches

TWO NUMBERS EVERY WORKLOAD NEEDS
RPO · Recovery point objective
How much recent data you can afford to lose, measured back from the moment of failure to the last copy in the secondary. “We can lose at most 15 minutes of orders.”
RTO · Recovery time objective
How long the business can wait for the service to work again, measured forward from the failure. “Finance dashboards back within 4 hours.”

Managed versus self-built disaster recovery

Both start from the same place: a second workspace and metastore in another region, with the same identities. The difference is who builds and runs the replication and the failover. Databricks recommends managed DR, and self-built DR for what it doesn't cover.

Managed disaster recovery
RECOMMENDED
Databricks runs the replication pipeline and the failover. You configure a failover group in the account console under Resilience.

Set RTO and RPO per workload
Not everything needs active-active. Agree targets with the business, then pick the cheapest strategy that meets them.
Back up as well as replicate
DR protects against losing a region. Delta history, Deep Clone backups and soft delete protect against losing data.
Rehearse every quarter
Run a planned failover, measure the real RTO and RPO, and fix what the test finds. The runbook and code change with every release.
